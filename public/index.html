How it works

Four quiet steps. Most people finish the first three in an evening and then forget about it, which is the idea.

1

Fill your vault

Logins, policies, where the papers are, who to call, what to cancel. A starter checklist covers what families most often can't find.

2

Choose Lightkeepers

Two to ten people you trust. Each gets a printed Keeper Card. You decide how many must come together to open the vault.

3

Send your signal

Once a month an email asks "still here?". One click. Miss it and you're reminded, gently and repeatedly, before anyone else hears a word.

4

Last Light

Only after a long silence are your Lightkeepers sent a link. They enter their codes together and read what you left, starting with your letter.

Why you can trust it

The server is designed to be useless to an attacker, and useless to us.

Encrypted before it leaves your browser

Your password never reaches the server. It derives two keys on your device: one logs you in, the other unlocks the vault, which is sealed with AES-256-GCM. The server stores only ciphertext.

Nobody can open it alone

The vault key is split with Shamir's secret sharing. Each Lightkeeper's piece is locked under a code that exists only on their printed card. The server can't open anything; neither can one person.

Warnings you can't miss

Reminders go to you first, every day, for a week. Then your Lightkeepers are only asked to check on you. The vault opens a full week after that, and never a minute sooner.

Open source, yours to run

About 250 lines of cryptography you can read in an hour, three dependencies, no build step, no analytics, no third-party requests. Run your own copy for twenty years.

Runs on hosting you already have

Last Light was built around what every Hostinger plan includes: a Node.js app, a MySQL database, a mailbox and a cron job. No VPS, no root access, no extra services.

Fork the repository, add a Node.js website from it in hPanel, paste the settings from .env.example, add one hourly cron line. About fifteen minutes.

Questions people ask

What if I forget my password?

Nobody can reset it, not even the server. That is the price of a server that knows nothing. Write the password down somewhere safe. Your Lightkeepers remain the only other way in, after a release.

Can my Lightkeepers read my vault whenever they like?

No. Before a release the server will not even hand out the sealed pieces, and even after a release the vault only opens when enough of them enter their codes together.

What if I'm just on holiday?

You choose how often to check in (7 to 365 days) and how long the grace period is. Missing a signal starts daily reminders to you, not an alarm to anyone else. Logging in from any device counts as a signal.

What do Lightkeepers need to be?

People who can find each other. A partner, a sibling, a close friend, an accountant. They need no technical skill: the retrieval page works on a phone and asks only for the code on their card.

Is it really free?

Yes. MIT licence, no accounts with us, no telemetry. You pay only for your own hosting and domain.
